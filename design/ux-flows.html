V1 UX Flows

The flow, the hierarchy, and what's primary versus secondary.

Passenger is one map. You open the app and you're looking at Tel Aviv, right now — how packed everywhere is, and whether each place feels local or touristy.

You drag a slider to see the next 12 hours, tap a neighborhood to read about it, tap a place and get handed off to Maps or Waze to actually walk there. You save places, and the app quietly remembers where you've actually been. Occasionally it asks you, in passing, whether a place felt local — because that's how the map gets smarter.

There is no feed, no profile, nothing to scroll. If you already know what you're after, search is one tap away — a sheet you open, not a box that's always staring back at you: a product whose whole pitch is "you don't need to ask" shouldn't put a question mark in front of you by default. Every screen is either the map, or something the map handed you.

Source: strategy/passenger-strategy.md (2026-07-27) + strategy/decisions.md (decisions 18–23). Markdown twin: design/ux-flows.md. Cross-feature flows reference, not a per-feature design spec — no PRDs exist yet to trace against.

The core of this doc

Feature hierarchy

Three tiers, ranked by how unavoidable each thing is. Cost is measured in taps from cold open — tapping the app icon is 0.

Primary Permanently on the map. The user cannot avoid seeing it.
The map0 taps Tel Aviv, MapKit, always the base layer. It's the whole product — strategy: "one map, and the whole product lives on it."
Heat layer0 taps Crowd-density fill in stepped bands, no gradients (decision #17). On by default, the first thing you see.
Tag layer0 taps Localness accent, three plain-language values: Local Mix Tourist — on by default, orthogonal to heat, never blended. Heat + tag is the entire V1 map — two layers, exactly as the north star describes. Renders at whatever granularity is legible per zoom, not everywhere at once — see Map state, revised after Aviran's pushback on badge density.
"Tel Aviv, right now" title0 taps Fading ambient label on cold open. Decision #8, verbatim.
Time slider0 to see · 1 drag DESIGN CALLNow → +12h, bottom-third thumb zone. Permanently visible, never dismissed, and it reshapes the primary view — that's Primary chrome, not a sheet you invoke. Overrulable in one line.
Category chips0 to see · 1 tap DESIGN CALLFood & drinks / Things to do. Same reasoning as the slider — always-visible chrome, not an invoked sheet.
Near-me button0 to see · 1 tap Recenter affordance. Persistent icon, part of map chrome.
Neighborhood button"See all of [Neighborhood]"0 to see at neighborhood zoom · 1 tap DESIGN CALLExplicit, Bump-inspired affordance naming the dominant zone in view — a second, more reliable door into the same zone sheet a polygon tap already opens. Map-surface chrome, not a sheet, so it belongs with Primary by construction — but it's the first conditional Primary item here: visible only at neighborhood zoom, not from anywhere the way the slider or chips are. Exists because a loosely-bounded polygon is a hard-to-discover tap target (Fitts's Law) — tapping the zone shape still works.
Secondary Invoked from the map.
Lighter than it might otherwise be. V1 hands off to native Maps/Waze at the moment of "go" rather than building its own routing screen, so there's no in-app takeover surface to place here. Three items now, not two — search (decision #23) joins zone sheet and spot sheet.
Zone sheet1 tap Hand-curated neighborhood blurb + tagged spot list. Reached by tapping a zone shape or the neighborhood button when one's showing — two doors, same destination. Bottom sheet; the map stays visible behind it.
Spot sheet1–2 taps Name, category, vibe tag, save icon, "Go" button. One level under a zone sheet, or reachable directly from a close-zoom pin. "Go" hands off to native Maps/Waze — an exit from Passenger, not a screen inside it.
Search sheet1 tap Secondary, not Primary — Aviran's explicit call, and the reasoning matters: a permanent search bar sitting in front of a product whose whole pitch is "you don't need to ask" undercuts that pitch. One tap away, gone when you're done, keeps the map itself as the thing you look at rather than a results page waiting for a query. Matches place names, keywords, and neighborhoods.
Tertiary Opt-in, low-frequency. Doesn't block the core loop.
Saved places1 tap Bookmarked spots. Deliberately sought out, not part of the glance-and-go loop.
Visited places1 tap Read-only in V1. Populates automatically from geofence detection (decision #16) — no manual "mark as visited" action. Designed as a list you read, not a flow you drive.
Local-QA answering0 to see · 1 tap DESIGN CALLContextual micro-prompt asking whether a spot is actually local. It renders inside a Secondary surface, but carries no value to the person answering — pure goodwill, skippable, no incentive layer until Phase 3's points system. Tiered by importance-to-the-user, not by where it sits.
Location permission0 auto · 1 via near-me System sheet plus in-app fallback copy if denied. One-time, OS-owned, not app chrome.
Settingsn/a None exist in V1. No account, no preferences, no toggles beyond what's already Primary chrome.

Before any journey starts

Launch behavior

First launch ever

  1. Tap the app icon. No splash screen, no onboarding carousel — the scope gate is explicit: the app opens straight to the map plus the location permission.
  2. Map renders immediately. Tel Aviv, default city-wide center, heat layer for "now", tag accents visible, both categories shown, slider at "now". "Tel Aviv, right now" fades in and out over roughly two seconds.
  3. Location permission prompts lazily — the OS system sheet, never a custom in-app screen (decision #8). It does not block map interaction.
    • Granted: map animates to real location, "you are here" marker appears, background Visited detection starts silently.
    • Denied: map stays at the default city-wide center — walked end to end in Journey 6.
  4. User explores. Pan, zoom, drag the slider, toggle a category, tap a zone or spot. The steady state almost every session lives in.

Every subsequent launch

  1. Tap the app icon. Map renders against whatever permission state iOS already recorded — no re-prompt.
  2. Granted: opens already centered on current location, no animate-in beat. Denied: opens at the same default city-wide center as first run.
  3. Slider always resets to "now." DESIGN CALLPersisting a stale "+3h" from last session would misrepresent live data the instant the app reopens. "Now" is only ever valid at the moment you look at it.

Six journeys, start to finish

End-to-end journeys

Chosen to partition V1's surface without duplicating it: two discovery contexts (tourist and resident, who use the same map differently), one return visit, one feedback loop, one search-first context, and one full pass under degraded conditions — because location-denied and offline don't touch one step, they touch nearly every step at once.

Search earns its own journey rather than folding into an existing one: every other journey starts with reading the map — a zone, a slider drag, a saved list — while search is the one path that starts with already knowing what you want and skips the reading entirely. It sits right before the degraded run: a normal alternate entry point, followed by the stress-test pass that touches everything that came before it, search included.

Every V1 interaction appears in at least one journey. Where something can go wrong, it's attached to the step where it bites.

Journey 1 · Discovery, cold

Just landed, knows nothing

A tourist, first time opening the app, has just landed in Tel Aviv.

  1. Taps the app icon. Map renders immediately — Tel Aviv, default center, heat + tag on for "now", both categories shown, "Tel Aviv, right now" fading in and out.
  2. A few seconds in, the OS location sheet appears without blocking the map underneath. She taps Allow — the map animates to her real location, a "you are here" marker appears, and Visited detection starts silently in the background.
  3. She taps the Things to do chip to cut the clutter. The map filters instantly.
  4. She taps a zone near her hotel. A bottom sheet slides up with a hand-curated blurb and a list of tagged spots. If there's no data hereThe sheet reads "Nobody's mapped this corner of Tel Aviv yet" instead of an empty list. She keeps browsing; nothing broke.
  5. She taps a spot in the list — a rooftop bar tagged Local, in a zone where heat is already climbing for this hour. Spot sheet opens: name, category, vibe tag, save icon, "Go".
  6. She taps Go. Passenger hands her straight to native Maps/Waze with the destination pre-filled — an exit, not a screen inside Passenger. She never sees an in-app route.
  7. She walks there using Maps/Waze; Passenger is backgrounded. If the geofence monitor catches her arrival, it silently logs a Visited entry. She doesn't see this happen — it's the setup for Journey 4.
Outcome Standing in front of the bar. 4 taps in-app
Journey 2 · Discovery, warm

Home and bored, planning tonight

A Tel Aviv resident, opening the app on a random Tuesday evening, deciding where to go later.

  1. Taps the app icon — not his first launch, so no permission re-prompt. Map opens centered on his location. Slider resets to "now", as it always does.
  2. He drags the slider forward to roughly +3 hours. Heat redraws live as he drags; the tag layer doesn't move — a place's localness doesn't change because it's later. Nothing there at that hourOne zone shows almost no heat at +3h. Not an error — real information, and exactly what he needed to see.
  3. He leaves both category chips on, since he's not sure what he wants yet.
  4. He taps a couple of zones back to back, comparing blurbs and tags, swiping each sheet down to bounce to the next.
  5. He finds a bar tagged Mix worth going to later, opens its spot sheet, and taps the save icon. It fills with a quick animation, an inline "Saved" appears, and he stays put. Connection drops mid-saveIt still saves locally and syncs once he's back online. A save is too lightweight to gate on connectivity.
Outcome A saved place and a plan for later. Journey 3 picks up from here.
Journey 3 · Return visit

Coming back to something saved

Same resident, a few hours later, ready to actually go.

  1. Opens the app, taps the Saved icon.
  2. Saved list opens; he taps the bar from Journey 2.
  3. The row jumps straight to that spot's sheet, skipping the zone sheet entirely — he already chose this place.
  4. The spot sheet re-reads current data: heat may have shifted since he saved it (different hour now); the vibe tag hasn't. Gap in current-hour dataThe sheet still opens with its static info — name, category, blurb — and the heat readout says "no live data right now" rather than blocking the sheet.
  5. He taps Go — same hand-off exit as Journey 1, straight to native Maps/Waze.
Outcome Standing in front of the bar — shorter than Journey 1 on purpose. Re-finding a place you already chose shouldn't cost as much as discovering one. 3 taps in-app
Journey 4 · Feedback loop

Giving something back

Either the tourist from Journey 1 or the resident from Journey 3, sometime after actually visiting a place.

  1. Because location was granted and the geofence monitor detected her presence, the spot from Journey 1 has quietly appeared in her Visited list. No action was required to put it there — Visited is automatic, not a manual button (decision #16).
  2. She opens the Visited icon out of curiosity. Nothing detected yetReads "Nothing here yet — this fills in as you walk around Tel Aviv" rather than a blank screen.
  3. She taps the entry, which reopens that spot's sheet. Embedded at the bottom is a plain-language micro-prompt: "Does this feel like a local spot, or more of a tourist one?" with three tap targets — Local Mix Tourist — the same three words used everywhere else in the app. This is the highest-intent moment to ask: she's already been there, and answering costs nothing beyond a tap she was already making.
  4. She taps Local. The card collapses into a one-line "Thanks — that's shared with other travelers." No modal, no interruption. OfflineThe card doesn't render at all, rather than offering a submit that silently fails. This system runs on goodwill alone in V1, and a failed submission burns that goodwill for nothing. Already answeredThe card never appears again for that spot.
Outcome One data point fed back into the localness pipeline, at zero extra cost.
Journey 5 · Search-first

I already know what I'm looking for

A resident whose friend just texted "go to Port Said" — or anyone chasing a specific craving, not interested in browsing.

  1. Taps the search icon in map chrome. A sheet opens over the map with a single text field — no default suggestions needed to start.
  2. Types "Port Said." Matches appear as she types. The same field matches three kinds of things: place names (this one), keywords ("hummus," "rooftop bar"), and neighborhoods ("Florentin"). No results"Nothing matching 'Port Said' right now" — the field stays open and editable. Same empty-state convention used everywhere else: a line, not a dead end.
  3. She taps the place-name result. The search sheet transitions directly into that spot's sheet — name, category, vibe tag, save icon, "Go". The tag and heat reflect the current slider hour, exactly as if she'd tapped the pin on the map; search filters into the same live data, it doesn't invent a separate result-only view. Result exists, no data at this hourHeat reads "no live data right now" — same treatment as a saved place with a data gap (Journey 3). Search doesn't get its own rule for this.
  4. She taps Go — hands off to native Maps/Waze exactly like every other spot sheet. Search doesn't create a second kind of exit.
  5. Alternate ending — a neighborhood result: if she'd typed "Florentin" instead, selecting it pans the map there and opens that zone's sheet — the same blurb-plus-spot-list surface as tapping the zone directly, just reached from a query instead of a glance. Neighborhood has no curated blurb yetSame empty state as tapping an under-curated zone from the map — search surfaces the zone, it doesn't invent content for it.
  6. OfflineSearch runs against whatever's already cached locally — matches are limited to that, and anything opened from a result carries the same "last updated Xm ago, offline" label used everywhere else.
Outcome At the spot sheet (or zone sheet) — cheaper than either discovery journey, because there was no browsing to do. 2 taps in-app
Journey 6 · Degraded

The degraded run

Anyone, location denied and/or offline for the whole session.

  1. Cold open with location denied (or offline entirely). Map renders at the default Tel Aviv city-wide center — no recenter, no "you are here" marker.
  2. The near-me button stays visible but greyed. Tapping it doesn't re-trigger the system dialog — iOS won't, once denied — so it shows inline copy pointing to Settings instead.
  3. She browses anyway. The map is fully usable without location, just not personalized to where she's standing. She taps a zone. Network also downThe sheet shows the last cached blurb and spot list with a "last updated Xm ago, offline" label rather than failing blank.
  4. She taps a spot and saves it — the save still completes locally and syncs once she's back online.
  5. She checks the Visited list. Permanently empty, with an explainer ("Turn on location to build this automatically") and a Settings deep-link. Visited has no data source without location — ever, not just today.
  6. She opens a spot sheet. The local-QA card never renders here; it depends on both a live connection and a real detected visit, neither of which this session can produce.
  7. She taps the search icon anyway — location denial doesn't touch it (search was never location-scoped to begin with), but offline shrinks it to whatever's cached, same as Journey 5's offline path.
  8. She taps Go anyway. The hand-off to Maps/Waze still works offline — it's just handing coordinates to another app, not asking anything of Passenger's servers.
Outcome She can still browse, filter, search, read blurbs and tags, save places, and get routed out. What she loses: personalization, a live Visited list, and any chance to answer a local-QA question. Nothing crashes and nothing lies to her about data being fresher than it is — that's the bar, not full feature parity.

Getting around without a nav bar

Navigation model

No nav bar, no tab bar, no feed. Two surface types, and one exit.

Map chrome

Always on screen, never dismissed — heat and tag layers, slider, category chips, fading title, near-me button, Saved and Visited icons. The neighborhood button lives here too, with one difference: it's conditionally present, only rendering at neighborhood zoom, rather than visible from any state the way the rest of this list is.

Sheets

Partial height, swipe down or tap outside to dismiss — zone sheet, spot sheet, search sheet, Saved list, Visited list. The local-QA card is embedded inside the spot sheet, not a sheet of its own.

"Go" is not a surface at all

It's a system hand-off to native Maps/Waze, which exits Passenger entirely. Returning afterward drops the user wherever iOS left off — typically the spot sheet or the map. Passenger doesn't reconstruct any state for this, because it never built a screen to leave in the first place.

The zone sheet now has three doors, one destination. Tapping a zone shape, tapping the neighborhood button, and selecting a neighborhood result from search all open the exact same zone sheet — none of them a different surface, none changing what depth costs. The button and search just give an always-there polygon tap two more reliable front doors.

Depth rule: 2 levels, no more, while inside the app

Map (0) → zone sheet (1) → spot sheet (2) is the deepest path that stays inside Passenger. Saved and Visited reach the same maximum by a shorter route, skipping the zone step because you already chose the place.

Search holds at the same ceiling, but gets there two different ways depending on the result type — worth confirming rather than assuming, since it isn't the same shortcut in both directions. Search (1) → spot sheet (2), for a place-name or keyword result, matches the Saved/Visited pattern exactly — it skips the zone-tap step because the user already named what they want. Search (1) → zone sheet (2), for a neighborhood result, isn't a shortcut at all — it reaches the same zone sheet a direct map tap would reach at level 1, just one level deeper, because the query itself occupies level 1 first. Either way, 2 stays the ceiling.

Nothing in V1 needs a third in-app level. The one feature that would have required it — Scenic View — is Phase 2, and keeping it out is exactly what holds this rule at 2 instead of 3.

Dismissing any sheet always returns exactly one level up.

What the map shows, when

Map state & density

Revised this roundAviran's pushback, verbatim: "how do you show tag layer on every location on the map? its gonna be too much information on one layer." He was right — the previous version badged every close-zoom pin, then stacked a warning badge on top of that. Worst case (Florentin at 8pm, dozens of pins) was heat fill plus a tag badge plus sometimes a warning badge, once per pin, all at once. Rewritten below around one governing fix: tag gets the same progressive disclosure heat already has, instead of trying to render at every zoom simultaneously.

Zoom levels, and where each layer actually lives

  • City-wide — heat as neighborhood-scale blobs. No tag signal at all yet, and no pins. Badging every neighborhood in Tel Aviv at once would be the identical clutter problem one level up.
  • Neighborhood — zone boundaries visible, heat as zone-level stepped-band fill. This is the tag layer's home. Each zone carries at most one small tag badge anchored at its centroid — Local, Tourist, or nothing (see the Mix rule below). Decision #12 bounds this at dozens of neighborhoods citywide and only a handful visible per viewport at this zoom — a legible badge count, which dozens of spot pins never were. Still no spot pins.
  • Close — individual spot pins appear. Pins carry no tag signal at all. Heat continues exactly as it rendered at neighborhood zoom; pins exist only to mark a location and its category. Tapping a pin opens the spot sheet, where the vibe tag renders as what it always was underneath the map: a word in a sheet, not a decoration competing with dozens of others.

DESIGN CALLWhether spot pins should carry tag at all was left open for me to decide. I'm calling it no, never — not "no badge, but a tag-colored pin shape instead." Even a pin whose own shape and fill encode tag still asks the eye to individually parse every pin in a crowded block one at a time; the only fix that survives Florentin at 8pm is moving spot-level tag off the map surface entirely, onto the zone sheet's list, where it's a word read one row at a time. Decision #12 still keeps spot-level localness data — this only changes where it renders. Pin anatomy, clustering, and exact labels are in the companion spec linked below.

Slider hours

Unchanged by any of this — heat is the only time-variant layer, tag isn't, so a zone's tag badge never moves when the slider does, regardless of which granularity it's currently rendering at.

Mix renders no badge, ever, at any granularity

With three tags, Mix is the unmarked middle and almost certainly the most common single value — badging it would be pure clutter carrying zero information. A zone badge only ever appears for Local or Tourist; a blank zone reads as Mix. DESIGN CALLA zone with no curated data yet also renders blank, identically — the map surface doesn't try to distinguish "confidently Mix" from "not yet rated," and that's an accepted trade-off: tapping in always resolves which one it is, the same way every other empty state in this doc resolves "no data here" on contact rather than pre-announcing it on the map.

The packed + touristy trap — replace, don't stack

A zone that's simultaneously busy and Tourist-tagged doesn't get two decorations — it gets one badge, in a distinct warning form, replacing the plain Tourist badge outright. Never two elements competing for the same handful of square millimeters (Von Restorff: only one "special" element per view). Busy + Local still never gets a warning treatment of any kind — the absence stays legible precisely because the warning exists for the other case, and that logic survives the move to zone granularity unchanged. The "very local but temporarily busy" case holds too: heat is time-variant, tag isn't, so a Local-tagged zone spiking busy at 9pm reads as "busy AND local," never as evidence it turned touristy.

Pin anatomy, clustering & accessibility labels

Specified in the companion doc, not here — that level of rendering detail would bloat a flows document: design/map-rendering-spec.md (markdown only, no reading-view twin). What belongs here is the structural fact that governs it: the busy+Tourist warning and the Mix-is-silent rule both now live at zone badges, not spot pins.

Search results and the map — a real design call, unaffected by the above

Decision #23 settles that results carry heat and tag and honor the slider hour, but not what the map itself does visually while the search sheet is open.

DESIGN CALLWhile results are showing, the map underneath dims everything except the matching pins or zones — search filters what's visually prominent, not just what's technically attached to each result row, so "search filters the map, it doesn't bypass it" is true on screen, not only in the data. This is temporary: the moment a result is selected and the destination sheet opens, the dimming clears and the full unfiltered heat/tag view returns underneath. Search doesn't become a second, persistent filter sitting alongside the category chips — that would blur the one thing category chips are for and undercut the same "you don't need to ask" reasoning that keeps search out of Primary chrome in the first place.

Placement only, not design

Where Phase 2 and 3 features slot in

Scenic View and Live Events both moved to Phase 2 as of this revision, subscription-gated alongside proximity intelligence.

Phase 2 · changes the rules Scenic View Replaces V1's "Go" hand-off with a full-screen in-app routing surface. Not just an addition — it changes what leaving the spot sheet means. In V1, Go ends the in-app journey, with nothing to reconstruct on return. With Scenic View, the journey continues inside Passenger during transit, which reopens two things V1 sidesteps entirely: what re-entering the spot context looks like after arrival (V1 has no "you've arrived" moment, since the app was never watching), and the depth rule, which goes back to 3 levels the moment this ships. Worth scoping alongside proximity intelligence — both concern the in-transit experience and would likely share build surface. The open depth question (full turn-by-turn vs. route-preview-then-handoff) travels with it.
Phase 2 · Primary Live Events Enters as a third overlay toggle alongside the category chips — additive chrome, since it extends the base map's visualization rather than opening a new surface. Displaces nothing. The two-layer hero view doesn't need to change shape to gain a third toggle later, which is exactly why cutting Events from V1 costs nothing structurally now.
Phase 2 · Secondary Proximity intelligence + arrival card A new Secondary surface, but geofence-triggered rather than tap-invoked — a time-triggered variant of the spot sheet, appearing when the user is already en route. Extends the spot sheet; displaces nothing.
Phase 3 · structural AI local guide A different product mode on the same engine, not a sheet off the map. Needs its own Primary-adjacent entry point. The one candidate that breaks V1's single-primary-surface simplicity rather than extending it. Flag it loudly whenever Phase 3 gets scoped.
Phase 3 · Secondary Shake to roll A gesture-triggered action roughly parallel to the spot sheet — a random-suggestion overlay fired by a device gesture instead of a tap. Purely additive.
Phase 3 · Tertiary Auto-saved places Extends the save step in Journey 2 with an automatic trigger (dwell ≥ 20 min). No new surface — Saved gains an automatic path alongside the manual one.
Phase 3 · Tertiary Points system Adds a rewards surface and changes the character of Journey 4. Local-QA is goodwill-only today and correctly tiered low. Once points exist, answering has real incentive behind it and deserves re-tiering upward — at that point, not before.

Decisions this doc couldn't make

Open UX questions

  1. Q1 Local-QA prompt cadence

    The strategy says the QA system exists, but not how often or on what trigger it asks.

    Recommendation: trigger primarily right after a spot lands in Visited — the highest-intent, already-there moment — plus occasionally on spot-sheet view for low-confidence tags only, capped at roughly once per session. Protect the goodwill this runs on rather than maximizing answer volume.

  2. Q2 Lazy location permission — exact trigger

    Decision #8 says "lazy," but not whether that's an automatic system prompt shortly after the map renders, or only on the first tap of near-me.

    Recommendation: auto-prompt once, softly, a couple of seconds after the map first renders. Starts Visited tracking as early as possible without blocking the first look at the map.

  3. Q3 Does the slider ever look backward?

    Taken here as strictly forward-only, now → +12 hours. Worth confirming there's no case for "it was busier here 30 minutes ago" before this locks into the build.

  4. Q4 Visited detection during the hand-off

    V1's Visited list depends entirely on the geofence monitor catching a visit while Passenger is backgrounded — the user is inside Maps/Waze, not Passenger, at the exact moment they arrive. Does iOS reliably keep background location running through that hand-off and the walk that follows, or does exiting the app risk losing the one signal Visited depends on?

    Recommendation: confirm background-location behavior with the architect before treating Visited's automatic-only design as settled. If it's shaky, a lightweight manual backstop may be needed even though the current decision rules one out.

  5. Q5 Does the busy + Tourist warning badge (now zone-level) need its own VoiceOver label?

    It's a display-time computation, not a stored tag, so it won't inherit whatever label the plain Tourist badge already carries.

    Recommendation: give it an explicit label — "busy and touristy, worth a second look" — rather than relying on VoiceOver to read heat and tag separately and expecting the combination to be inferred. This now has a sharper edge: the Mix-is-silent rule means a sighted user reads "no badge" as Mix, but VoiceOver can't read an absence — resolved in full in design/map-rendering-spec.md's accessibility section, kept coherent with this question rather than answered twice.

  6. Q6 Does the search sheet keep recent searches?

    There's no account and no persistence story anywhere else in V1 — the time slider itself resets to "now" every launch on purpose.

    Recommendation: default to no persisted history across launches, matching that pattern. A session-only recent list, cleared on relaunch, is a reasonable middle ground if a completely blank field on every open feels too cold — but that's worth Aviran's read, since locals searching the same handful of things repeatedly is a real, recurring use case this would help.

  7. Q7 Does search respect the active category filter?

    If a user has narrowed the map to "Food & drinks" and then searches a keyword that's ambiguous across categories (e.g., "market"), does search scope to the active chip or search everything regardless?

    Recommendation: search everything regardless of the active category filter — a typed query is a more specific signal of intent than a standing chip, and scoping it down risks silently hiding the exact result the user typed for.

  8. Q8 What does the neighborhood button do when the viewport straddles two zones roughly evenly?

    At the boundary between Florentin and Neve Tzedek, say, with no single dominant neighborhood in view.

    Recommendation: don't guess at a winner. Hide the button entirely below some clear-dominance threshold (e.g., one zone needs to cover meaningfully more than half the viewport) rather than risk sending someone into the wrong neighborhood's sheet with false confidence — tapping either zone shape directly still works regardless.

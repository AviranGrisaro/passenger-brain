# Customer Perspective Review: amp Connect

## My Situation

I bought an amp device. I use it at home a few times a week. I like the simplicity of it — pull the cable, see my weight, do my sets. I never downloaded the app because the device works fine without it. Now I'm reading that a software update is coming that will change how my device works.

---

## First Reaction: This Feels Like a Bait-and-Switch

I paid good money for this device. It showed me my weight, my reps, my resistance modes. Now you're telling me that after a firmware update, my dial will go blank and just show groove marks unless I download your app and keep my phone next to the device via Bluetooth every single time I work out?

That is not what I bought. When I purchased this device, it worked standalone. The product page, the ads, the unboxing — none of it said "requires app for basic display." You are taking away functionality I already had and calling it an "update." The PRD even acknowledges this is a risk and calls it "user backlash on existing devices (HIGH)." They know this will make people angry.

The suggested communications framing — leading with "safety and value" and calling it an "unlock" of new features — feels dishonest. You are not unlocking anything for me. You are locking what I already had and asking me to jump through hoops to get it back. Customers will see through this framing immediately.

---

## What Actually Happens to My Workout

Let me walk through what this means for my daily routine:

1. I walk up to my amp device to work out.
2. The dial no longer shows my weight or rep count. It just shows groove marks and a message telling me to connect the app.
3. I have to pull out my phone, open the amp app, make sure Bluetooth is connected.
4. Only then do my numbers come back on the dial.
5. If my phone battery dies mid-workout, or the Bluetooth drops, or the app crashes — my numbers disappear again. I have a 2-minute grace period before the dial goes blank.
6. If I forget my phone upstairs, I'm working out blind.

This is worse than what I have today. Today I walk up, pull the cable, and see my weight. Simple. The new experience adds friction to every single workout, and the only "benefit" to me is getting back what I already had.

---

## The App Requirement Is Annoying

I specifically chose not to download the app. Maybe I don't want another app on my phone. Maybe I don't want to deal with Bluetooth pairing issues. Maybe I value the simplicity of a standalone device. This update takes away my choice.

The PRD frames the device as "still working at full resistance" without the app. Technically true — I can still pull the cable. But working out without being able to see my weight or rep count is like driving a car where the speedometer is blacked out. Yes, the car still drives, but you've made it meaningfully worse.

---

## Subscription Pressure Is Transparent

Even after I download the app and connect it, I only get "fixed mode." The band and eccentric resistance modes I may have been using are now locked behind a subscription. So the real flow is:

1. You take away my dial numbers to force me to download the app.
2. You take away my resistance modes to force me to pay a subscription.
3. You frame both of these as "features" and "value."

This is a two-step squeeze. The PRD is explicit about this — it calls it a "two-step conversion funnel." That is honest internally, but from a customer perspective, it feels manipulative. I bought a device that did all of this for free. Now each piece is being gated behind a new requirement.

---

## Things I Would Actually Like

Not everything here is bad. Some parts of this would genuinely improve my experience IF they were offered as additions rather than replacements:

- **Muscle group tagging during freestyle**: This is useful. I would love to track which muscles I worked.
- **Workout suggestions during rest periods**: Actually a nice touch. If I'm resting between sets and the app surfaces a relevant guided workout, that is helpful — not intrusive.
- **The BLE tray showing "Active Workout"**: Fine as an informational feature. Knowing the device sees me and my workout is being tracked is reassuring.
- **Firmware updates for safety and calibration**: Completely reasonable. I want my device to be safe. But this can happen without locking my dial.

The problem is not that the app has value. It does. The problem is that you are removing standalone device functionality to force me toward the app. If you made the app compelling enough, I would download it voluntarily. The fact that you need a "forcing function" (the PRD's words) suggests the app is not compelling enough on its own.

---

## Questions and Concerns I Have as a Customer

**Will I get a refund?** The PRD lists a "legacy customer policy" as an unresolved decision. If you change what I bought after I bought it, I want to know my options. The fact that this is still undecided is concerning — you're planning to ship something that changes my device before you've decided what to do about the people who paid for the current experience.

**What about when Bluetooth is unreliable?** Bluetooth is not perfect. I've had pairing issues with other devices. If BLE drops during my workout, my dial goes blank after 2 minutes. That is a terrible experience during an intense set. The PRD treats BLE reliability as a given, but it is not.

**What if I don't have my phone?** Sometimes I work out without my phone nearby. Under the new system, I cannot see my weight at all. The PRD does not address this scenario with any empathy — it treats it purely as a "forcing function" success case.

**What about older users or less tech-savvy customers?** Not everyone is comfortable with QR codes, Bluetooth pairing, and app setup. The PRD does not mention accessibility or onboarding support for users who might struggle with the technical requirements.

**What about privacy?** The app now becomes mandatory. What data is it collecting? The PRD mentions Amplitude analytics, device serial numbers in URLs, and tracking of my workout behavior. If I didn't want an app tracking my workouts before, I now have no choice.

**Will the device ever work standalone again?** Is there a path back? Or is this permanent? The PRD mentions a "kill switch" for internal use, but there is no mention of a user-facing opt-out. I cannot choose to keep my old experience.

---

## What Is Missing from This PRD

**The customer's voice.** This PRD is written entirely from the business perspective. The problem statement is about revenue and conversion, not about customer needs. The user problem is framed as "users miss features they don't know about" — but maybe they don't want those features. Maybe they bought a simple device and like it simple.

**User research with device-only users.** The PRD notes that baseline data is pending from Itai. There is no mention of actually talking to device-only users to understand WHY they don't use the app. Maybe it's not awareness — maybe it's preference. Building a forcing function without understanding the reason for the behavior is risky.

**An opt-out for existing customers.** The PRD explicitly lists "user-controlled opt-out toggle" as a non-goal. This means existing customers who are happy with their current device experience have no way to keep it. That feels wrong.

**A genuine free tier.** Non-paying users who connect the app get fixed mode and freestyle only. There are no free guided workouts. The app experience for non-paying users is thin — you're forcing them to download something that immediately asks them to pay. The PRD acknowledges no free-tier workouts exist.

**Offline mode or fallback.** What happens if the user's phone is dead, out of battery, or they simply don't want to bring it to the workout? The device becomes a resistance cable with no display. There should be a local fallback or at minimum a discussion of how to handle this gracefully.

**Empathy in the rollout.** The graduated rollout plan is well-structured operationally, but it is entirely metric-driven. There is no mention of gathering qualitative customer sentiment beyond NPS numbers. Talking to actual users during the rollout stages would surface problems that ticket rates alone won't catch.

---

## Bottom Line

Would I use amp Connect? Reluctantly, yes — because I would have no choice. My dial would be useless without it. That is not the same as wanting it.

The features being offered (muscle tracking, workout suggestions, guided programs) are genuinely useful. But wrapping them in a forced app download and a subscription gate transforms what could be a compelling upgrade into something that feels coercive.

If amp offered this as "download the app to get these great new features, and your device keeps working as-is if you don't" — I would probably download it. The value proposition is real. But "download the app or lose your display" is not an invitation, it is an ultimatum.

The biggest risk here is not that customers won't download the app. They probably will, because they have no real alternative. The risk is that they will resent it, and that resentment will show up in NPS, in reviews, in word-of-mouth, and in brand perception. You can hit 70% app download and still lose the customer relationship.

My advice: ship the app features, market them well, make the value obvious — and let customers choose. If you must push for app adoption, consider a softer approach: show the numbers but add a small persistent banner on the dial saying "Get more with the amp app." That is guidance, not a lock.
